    Sources. Generative AI exposure: Brookings Institution, county estimates of jobs exposed to generative AI (exposure_share = exposed jobs ÷ total jobs). Labor distress: W.E. Upjohn Institute for Employment Research, Local Labor Market Distress Dataset, county prime-age employment rate and distress ranking.

    Classes. Exposure is split into county terciles (low < , high ≥ ). Distress uses the dataset's own thresholds: healthy ≥ 80% prime-age employment (Fully or Close to fully employed), marginal 76.5–80%, distressed < 76.5% (Distressed or Severely distressed). Valdez-Cordova, AK and Kalawao, HI have no exposure estimate; Loving, TX has no employment rate. These are shown in gray.

    Industry mix. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW), 2024 annual average employment by county, counted where the jobs are located (the same basis as the AI exposure measure). Connecticut uses 2023 because BLS now reports its planning regions instead of counties. Government is grouped by level (federal civilian, state, local), so public school teachers and public hospital staff appear under government, and the private education and health line covers private employers only. Active-duty military are not counted. When BLS withholds a sector to protect employer confidentiality, those jobs appear as "Not disclosed." "All jobs" pools every job in the selection, so large counties dominate; "Avg. county" weights each county equally. On the Largest industry map, a county is striped when the jobs BLS withheld exceed the gap between its top two sectors, so the withheld jobs could change which sector ranks first.
