    Methodology

    AI exposure Brookings Institution

    Brookings starts from occupation-level ratings by OpenAI researchers (Eloundou et al., 2023) of how much of each occupation's tasks a large language model could perform or substantially speed up. It matches the ratings to 738 occupation codes in the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics and averages them where several job titles share a code.

    AI-exposed jobs in a county is employment in each occupation multiplied by that occupation's rating, summed. It is a weighted count, not a headcount. AI exposure share is AI-exposed jobs divided by total jobs; across counties it runs from about 20% to 47%. Exposure measures overlap with AI capabilities as of 2023. It is not a forecast of job losses, since exposed work may be automated, made more productive, or both.

    Muro, Methkupally and Kinder, “The geography of generative AI’s workforce impacts will likely differ from those of previous technologies,” Brookings, Feb. 19, 2025.

    Labor market distress W.E. Upjohn Institute

    Distress is measured by the prime-age employment rate: the share of civilians aged 25–54 who have a job. Limiting it to this age group removes most of the effect of students and retirees, and unlike the unemployment rate it counts people who have stopped looking for work. Rates are from the American Community Survey, 2015–2019 five-year estimates. Upjohn's five levels, and how this dashboard groups them:

    Upjohn Institute, Local Labor Market Distress Dataset; Bartik, “Using Place-Based Jobs Policies to Help Distressed Communities” (2020). County population is also from this dataset.

    Industry mix Bureau of Labor Statistics

    Employment by industry is from the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, 2024 annual average (Connecticut, 2023). Jobs are counted where people work, not where they live. Government is grouped by level, so public school teachers and public hospital staff count as government; active-duty military is excluded. “Not disclosed” is employment BLS withholds to protect individual employers.

    All jobs pools every job in the selection, so large counties dominate; Avg. county weights each county's mix equally. On the Largest industry map, stripes mark counties where withheld jobs exceed the lead of the largest sector over the second.
